# Travel Booking Guide

**Policy ID:** POL-FIN-008
**Owner:** Finance

This guide explains *how* to book travel. *What* you can spend is set by the Expense Policy (POL-FIN-002).

## Before you book

1. Get your manager's approval for the trip in a Slack message or email (keep it; Spendly may ask for it).
2. For international travel, check visa requirements at least **6 weeks** ahead. The People team can provide invitation letters.
3. Check whether the trip could be a video call instead.

## Booking in TripDesk

- All flights, hotels and rail should be booked in **TripDesk**, Acme's travel portal. TripDesk charges directly to the company account, so you don't pay out of pocket.
- TripDesk shows in-policy options first. Out-of-policy options are flagged and need a written reason.
- Book flights at least **14 days** in advance.
- Hotel caps: $250/night standard, $325/night in London, New York and San Francisco.

## If you can't use TripDesk

For example, if a conference hotel is only bookable through the event. Pay with your corporate card or personal card and claim in Spendly with the receipt.

## Ground transport

- Public transport, rideshare or taxis are all fine. Choose the sensible option for time and cost.
- Car rental: compact or midsize. Decline extra insurance; Acme's policy already covers it.
- Personal car mileage is reimbursed at the local government rate.

## Travel safety

- TripDesk sends your itinerary to Acme's travel-risk provider automatically.
- Before travelling to a country with an elevated travel advisory, get approval from your VP and the People team.
- In an emergency, call the 24/7 travel assistance number on the back of your corporate card or in TripDesk.

## Combining business and personal travel

You may extend a business trip for personal reasons. Acme only pays for the costs it would have paid for the business trip alone. Extra nights and personal flights are at your own cost.

## Changes and cancellations

Make changes in TripDesk so the booking stays linked to the company account. Fees from changes caused by business needs are reimbursable; fees from personal changes are not.
