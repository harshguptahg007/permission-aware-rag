# Performance Improvement Plan (PIP) Guidelines

**For engineering managers only.**

A PIP is a last structured attempt to help someone succeed. It is not a formality before dismissal, and it should never be the first time someone hears about a problem.

## Before a PIP

You must have:
1. Raised the specific concern in at least **two documented 1:1s** over at least **6 weeks**.
2. Agreed clear, written expectations and given time to meet them.
3. Talked to your **People partner** (for engineering, currently Carol Diaz).
4. Checked for anything that could explain the performance (health, leave, workload, team changes). If so, talk to the People team first.

## The plan

- Length: **60 days** (30 days only in exceptional cases, with Head of People approval).
- 3–5 **specific, measurable** goals tied to the role's level expectations.
- Weekly check-ins, documented in PeopleHub.
- Support you'll provide (pairing, reduced scope, mentoring).

## Outcomes

| Outcome | What happens |
|---|---|
| Goals met | PIP closes; normal performance management continues |
| Clear progress but not all goals met | Extend once by up to 30 days |
| Goals not met | People partner and director review; may lead to termination |

## Rules

- PIPs need approval from your **director and People partner** before you start.
- Don't start a PIP for someone on, or within 4 weeks of returning from, protected leave without Legal review.
- Don't mention a PIP in team channels or to other team members.
- Rating 1 in a review cycle usually leads to a PIP; rating 2 usually leads to an informal development plan.

## Termination

Terminations are handled by the People team and Legal. Managers never terminate on their own. Severance follows the standard People team guidelines for the employee's country.
